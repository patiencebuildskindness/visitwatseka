Downtown Watseka Trail Crawl & Sidewalk Sale

Hubbard Trail Days

Retrace the path that built our town, one shop and one stamp at a time.

Saturday, September 12, 2026 11 AM – 5 PM Downtown Watseka

A shopping day built on a history lesson

Carry a passport from shop to shop, collect a stamp at every stop, and work your way toward the prize drawings. Along the way you find out whose trail you have been walking your whole life. No lecture, no admission, just twenty good reasons to walk the street.

Two Ways to Play

Paper or phone, your choice. Both count exactly the same.

1

Get your passport

Pick up a paper passport at the Santa House, or use the digital one on your phone. Both count exactly the same.

2

Collect your stamps

Step inside each participating shop, look around, and ask for your stamp. Every stamp counts toward a drawing.

3

Turn it in and win

Bring your passport back to the Santa House. A volunteer checks your stamps and enters you on the spot.

On Paper

The printed passport

Pick one up at the Santa House in the First Trust parking lot. That is also where you finish: bring your stamped passport back, and a volunteer will tell you which drawing you have earned and take your entry right there. The passport is yours to keep.

On Your Phone

The digital passport

Would rather not carry a booklet? Open the digital passport and scan the QR code at the counter of any participating business to collect that stamp automatically. Same twenty stops, same stamps, same drawings.

Open the Digital Passport

The more stamps, the better the drawing

Three tiers, based on how many stamps you gather. Drawings are held in the days following the event and winners are contacted directly.

Why “Hubbard”?

Two hundred years ago a young fur trader named Gurdon Hubbard set up a trading post where the Iroquois River meets Sugar Creek, the ground Downtown Watseka stands on today. The Potawatomi called him Pa-pa-ma-ta-be, or Swift-Walker, after he covered seventy-five miles on foot through the night to warn settlers of a threatened raid, arriving before dawn.

The path he wore north toward Chicago became known as Hubbard’s Trace. Settlers followed it, it widened into a road, and that road is now Illinois Route 1, still running straight through our downtown. Every storefront here sits on a two-hundred-year-old trade route.

But our town does not carry his name. It carries hers. Watchekee, “Daughter of the Evening Star,” was a Potawatomi woman and the daughter of the respected leader Shabbona. She married Hubbard here in 1826. After the 1833 Treaty of Chicago forced her people west, she walked back to this land again and again, thousands of miles over a lifetime of removals. In 1865, Middleport was renamed Watseka in her honor.

Most people here, visitors and lifelong residents alike, have never heard that story. Come collect it one stamp at a time.
