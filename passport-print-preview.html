Hubbard Trail Days — Passport (print master)

This is a half-fold booklet: one sheet of Letter paper, printed double-sided and folded once down the middle into a 5.5 × 8.5″ pocket passport.

  1. Press Ctrl/Cmd + P. Set paper to Letter, orientation Landscape, margins None, and turn on Background graphics.
  2. Choose Print on both sides and flip on short edge. (If the inside prints upside-down vs. the cover, switch to flip on long edge and reprint.)
  3. Fold the printed sheet in half along the center line. Cover on the outside — done.

The Trail Beneath Your Feet

The trail we walk — and the woman we’re named for

Two hundred years ago a young fur trader named Gurdon Hubbard — called Pa-pa-ma-ta-be, “Swift-Walker,” by the Potawatomi — traded where the Iroquois River meets Sugar Creek, the ground Downtown Watseka stands on today. The path he wore north toward Chicago became Hubbard’s Trace — now Illinois Route 1, still running through our downtown.

But our town carries a Potawatomi name, not his. Watchekee — “Daughter of the Evening Star” — was the daughter of the respected Potawatomi leader Shabbona, known for her intelligence and beauty. She married Hubbard here in 1826; their union lasted only a few years, but her story reached far beyond it.

Removed west after the 1833 Treaty of Chicago, Watchekee walked back to this land again and again — thousands of miles on foot across a lifetime of forced removals. In 1865 the town of Middleport was renamed Watseka in her honor.

Today you retrace that trail in miniature — one shop, one stamp at a time.

How your passport works
  1. Step inside, shop around, and collect a stamp at each stop.
  2. Fill your passport — every stamp counts.
  3. Check it in at the Santa House to be entered in the drawing (below).

Collect & Win

Bring your stamped passport back to the Santa House in the First Trust parking lot — right where you picked it up. We’ll check your stamps, tell you which drawing you’ve earned, and enter you on the spot. The more stamps you collect, the more you can win. Prizes to be announced.
